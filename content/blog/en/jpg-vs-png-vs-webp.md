When saving an image, choosing the right file extension can be confusing. Three widely used formats online are JPG (or JPEG), PNG, and WebP. Each has a distinct purpose, and choosing the wrong one leads to blurry text, oversized files, or lost transparency.

No single format suits every task. The right choice depends on your subject and destination. A format suitable for a personal blog may be rejected by an official application portal.

## The short answer

If you need a direct decision, use these guidelines:

* Photo for sharing, email, or online forms: Choose JPG. It keeps files compact and is widely supported on websites and devices.
* Screenshot, text scan, logo, or sharp graphic: Choose PNG. It protects letter clarity and avoids fuzzy compression artifacts.
* Need for a transparent background: Choose PNG or WebP. JPG does not support transparency.
* Pictures for your own website or blog: Choose WebP. WebP was made for the web and is a common choice for a site you control.
* Strict application portals: Choose JPG. WebP is not accepted everywhere, so check portal rules first.

## Understanding JPG, PNG, and WebP

Each format manages image data differently. Here is what each format is good for and where it falls short.

### JPG (JPEG)

JPG is a lossy format built for photographs and smooth gradients. It lowers file size by discarding subtle color variations that human eyes rarely notice. The key limitation is lack of transparency; when you save a transparent image as JPG, clear areas are filled with a solid color (in our tool, white). Lossy compression also creates artifacts around text, high-contrast borders, and line drawings.

### PNG

PNG is a lossless format designed to preserve every pixel. It keeps sharp lines and small text crisp, with full alpha channel support for transparent backgrounds. The trade-off is file weight. On camera photos, PNG files are very large and use far more storage than a JPG of the same subject.

### WebP

WebP is a versatile format built for the web. It supports lossy and lossless compression, and retains transparency in both modes. However, outside modern web browsers, WebP is not accepted everywhere. Some portals, forms and older programs do not accept WebP files.

## How to choose in 4 situations

Here is how to select your format across four common everyday situations:

### 1. A photo for a form upload

When uploading a passport photo, student card, or job application picture, choose JPG. Many government and exam portals ask for JPG or JPEG, so follow the instructions of the portal you are using. ### 2. A screenshot or document scan with text

When capturing error messages, receipts, or documents with text, choose PNG. Small characters require crisp contrast against the background. Lossy compression causes color halos around fine typography, reducing readability. PNG keeps every letter sharp and readable.

### 3. A logo or image with a transparent background

When preparing a logo, watermark, badge, or icon for varying backgrounds, choose PNG. Because JPG lacks transparency, saving a transparent graphic as JPG fills the clear area with solid white. PNG preserves transparency so your design blends naturally anywhere.

### 4. Pictures for a website

When adding pictures to your own website, portfolio, or blog, WebP is a dependable choice. Managing your own pages lets you use WebP to keep file sizes moderate while retaining clear visuals. Keep uncompressed originals on your computer if you need another format later.

## Common mistakes to avoid

* Converting a JPG to PNG does not restore lost detail. Once compression artifacts exist in a JPG, saving as PNG merely places those same pixels in a heavier file without improving clarity.
* Saving a transparent PNG as a JPG produces a permanent white background. Once transparent areas turn white during JPG export, you cannot recover transparency from that file. Always keep your original PNG safe.
* Trying to shrink a PNG photo through dimensions alone. Camera photos in PNG format remain heavy because PNG is lossless. Converting the photo to JPG or WebP often gives a smaller file without changing the pixel size.

## Final recommendations

Choose JPG for general photos and form submissions, PNG for screenshots, text, and transparent graphics, and WebP for your own website where you control the pages.
