Many forms and email systems ask for JPG files. A PNG can turn out heavier than expected, causing upload problems. Changing the format to JPG is often the fix.

You can complete this switch in a few clicks inside your browser. No software installation is required.

## When it makes sense, and when to keep the PNG

Switching from PNG to JPG changes how image data is stored. Converting to JPG is helpful for many tasks, while keeping the original PNG is preferable in other situations.

Convert to JPG when:
* A form or portal asks for JPG (many do, so follow the instructions of the one you use).
* Your image is a photo or camera shot and the file is heavy.
* You do not need a transparent background.

Keep the original PNG when:
* Your graphic is a logo, icon, or badge that needs a transparent background.
* You have a screenshot or document scan where tiny text must stay crisp.

## What happens to transparent areas

The main structural difference between the two formats is transparency. PNG files support transparent pixels, while JPG does not.

When you convert a transparent PNG, the tool draws it onto a canvas and fills transparent regions with solid white. The converter has no background color picker, so transparent areas always become white. Because this white fill becomes permanent pixel data in the JPG, you cannot make it transparent again simply by renaming the file. Keep your original PNG safe on your computer.

## What stays the same, what changes

Knowing what changes during conversion prevents surprises:

* Pixel dimensions stay identical. Width and height in pixels do not change unless you enter an optional target size.
* File name is retained. The downloaded file keeps your original name, updating only the extension to .jpg.
* Metadata is omitted. Camera details (EXIF data) are not kept during browser export.
* Compression becomes lossy. JPG discards subtle color data. Depending on the picture the difference may be small, but tiny letters and sharp edges can look softer.

## Common problems

Keep these three common points in mind:

* The background turned white. JPG does not support transparency, so clear areas turn solid white during conversion. Keep the PNG if you require transparent graphics.
* Small text looks softer. Lossy compression can create slight color halos around fine letters. If you have screenshots or documents where tiny typography must stay sharp, keep the original PNG.
* The JPG is not much smaller for a simple graphic. Simple graphics with few colors are already compact in PNG. Converting them to JPG can yield little difference in file weight. Check the New Size indicator on the page, and if you need a smaller file, try the target size field.
