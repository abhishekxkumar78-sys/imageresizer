Picture this: you are filling in a government form, the photo upload field rejects your file with a red error that says it exceeds 300 KB, and your scan is 4 MB. You search for an online compressor, but just before uploading, you wonder whether it is a good idea to send a scan of your ID to a server you know nothing about.

That is the problem we wanted to solve. We wanted simple, dependable image utilities that do their job fast without sending your personal pictures to our servers.

## Everything runs inside your browser

Almost every popular image editor on the web works the same way. You upload your photo, their server processes it, and you download the result back. That approach works, but it comes with real drawbacks. Your files sit on someone else's disks, uploads eat mobile data, and slow connections turn a two-second job into a chore.

We took a different path. Everything on this site runs directly on your computer or phone using modern browser technology. When you select an image, your browser decodes the pixels, applies the edits in memory, and gives you the finished file right away.

Nothing ever leaves your device. No uploads, no waiting in remote queues, and no risk of sensitive photos sitting on a database.

## The tools you can use right now

We kept the layout clean so you can get in, finish what you need, and get on with your day. Here is what is available today:

* [Image Resizer](/): Change pixel dimensions or scale by percentage while preserving proper proportions.
* [Crop Image](/crop): Cut out unwanted borders, frame faces, or match standard social media aspect ratios.
* [Image Compressor](/compress): Reduce file weight in JPG, PNG, and WebP formats without turning your photo into a blurry mess.
* [Format Converter](/convert): Switch between common image formats like PNG, JPG, and WebP in seconds.
* [Watermark Adder](/watermark): Stamp custom text watermarks onto your graphics before posting them online.
* [Image to PDF](/image-to-pdf): Combine one or dozens of photos into a single neat PDF file, with custom page order and margins.

## Handling PDFs and target file sizes

A lot of everyday paperwork is PDF, not photos: scanned receipts, contracts, multi-page forms. So we expanded three of our tools to accept PDF files too.

You can now drop full PDF documents directly into [Image Compressor](/compress) to shrink their total size before emailing them. PDF compression works by turning each page into an image, so selectable text is lost, and text-only PDFs may not get smaller. It suits scanned or image-heavy documents best. You can open a document in [Watermark Adder](/watermark) to place a confidential or draft stamp across every page. And inside [Image to PDF](/image-to-pdf), you can mix regular images with existing PDF pages, reorder them by dragging thumbnails around, and export a clean consolidated document.

We also added a target file size feature to the [Image Resizer](/), [Image Compressor](/compress), [Format Converter](/convert), [Watermark Adder](/watermark) (images only, not PDFs) and [Image to PDF](/image-to-pdf) tools. Instead of guessing quality sliders and hoping the output is small enough, you can type in an exact limit, such as 200 KB or 2 MB. The tool adjusts the compression, and for images also the dimensions if needed, to get as close to that limit as it can. Keep in mind that very large PDFs with many pages can take a bit longer, so give your device a moment to finish.
